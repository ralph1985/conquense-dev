## Cybersecurity needs places to test

The addition of CrowdStrike to Castilla-La Mancha’s Regional Digital Innovation Centre reinforces a useful idea: security improves when organisations can validate tools and procedures before relying on them in production. CRID acts as a meeting point for public administration, companies, training and technology.

The announced collaboration combines detection, threat intelligence and applied artificial intelligence capabilities. For a technical team, the value is not collecting products. It is checking how they fit with the inventories, identities, logs, backups and response processes already in place.

## From alert to decision

A platform can identify anomalous patterns across devices and services, but an isolated alert is not yet a defence. Teams need to identify the affected asset, estimate scope, preserve evidence and decide whether to block, isolate or observe. Automation helps save time when its actions are reversible and auditable.

AI can organise signals and reduce noise, although it introduces risks as well. A model can be wrong, learn from incomplete data or create a false sense of coverage. Escalation rules and human review remain necessary for incidents affecting important services.

## An opportunity for the regional fabric

The regional component matters because cybersecurity is not limited to large organisations. Small companies, healthcare centres and local administrations need proportionate practices: strong authentication, updates, segmentation, monitoring and tested recovery.

CRID can provide an environment for sharing knowledge and measuring results. If the collaboration leads to reproducible tests, trained professionals and faster responses, bringing in new actors will have an effect beyond any single brand.
