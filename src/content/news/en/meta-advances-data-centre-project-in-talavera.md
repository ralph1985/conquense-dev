## Infrastructure is part of the technology product

The urban-planning progress of Meta’s project in Talavera de la Reina is not yet an operating data centre, but it is a relevant signal about the infrastructure required by the digital economy. Parcel reorganisation and the next licensing steps turn a business intention into a project that must fit its surroundings.

A data centre is not evaluated only by the number of servers it can host. It needs redundant connectivity, stable power, cooling systems, physical security and procedures for uninterrupted operation. Each layer affects the ability to deliver web and AI services predictably.

## Latency, resilience and energy

Location has technical consequences. Being closer to users and companies can reduce latency for some services, while a region with available space may make growth easier. The real decision combines network, energy, water, land, permits and operating capacity over several decades.

Resilience is not achieved by duplicating machines in the same room. Teams must account for network failures, power cuts, configuration mistakes and events affecting an entire area. Distributed services need to understand their dependencies and define what degradation is acceptable when one component is unavailable.

## The hidden cost of scale

Large infrastructure can attract investment and specialised jobs, but it also requires transparency about resource consumption and local integration. Processor and cooling efficiency, renewable energy use and heat reuse are engineering decisions with a public impact.

For Castilla-La Mancha, the project places Talavera on the map of European cloud infrastructure. The next criterion will not only be whether the centre is built, but whether its operation combines availability, efficiency and lasting technological benefits for the region.
